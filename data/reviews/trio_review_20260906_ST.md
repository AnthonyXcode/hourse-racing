## Section 8: Learnings

### What Worked
- **PASSing genuinely broken races.** R4, R7 and R10 all lost their bankers (9th, scratched, 12th). Three of four PASSes were correct on the merits.
- **The R3 model-failure diagnosis.** The `raceCard.ts:790` rating-bound bug (rating 142 rejected, defaulted to 60) was found and fixed. The MC was correctly judged unusable for that race.
- **Class-drop error detection.** Every horse flagged pre-race as a class-drop victim of the model (R4 #1, R10 #1) placed, as did the progressive 4yo the market preferred (R9 #5). **The diagnosis was right; the tickets did not act on it.**
- **R8's "both models agree" test.** The one race where banker selection was not a model/market argument was the one race that hit.
- **Scratching discipline.** R7's banker was scratched; the pre-race PASS condition anticipated exactly this.

### What Didn't Work
- **Banker strike rate 3/9 (33.3%)** against an assumed ~56–82%. In a 膽拖 structure this is fatal — six races lost before leg selection mattered.
- **Both banker overrides failed**, and the R6 one actively destroyed a placing banker.
- **PASSing R3 on takeout arithmetic.** The reasoning (covering all 10 combos containing the banker = guaranteed −25% EV) is structurally sound, but the actual dividend was **$217 against my modelled fair value of $35** for the 1-2-6 combination, because the 1.4 place favourite #3 PATCH OF STARS ran out of the frame. **A −25% expectation is not a −100% one; the PASS forfeited +$117.**
- **The EIPH vet penalty removed R2's winner.** #1 LIGHTNESS OF MUSIC took −3/−4 for a clearance 19 days out, dropped out of the pool, and **won at 2.3**. Rule 9 (no exclusion at odds ≤15) should arguably have protected it.
- **Preferring wider Strategy B tickets cost money on the one hit.** R8's $60 Strategy A ticket returned $514 versus $424 for the recommended $150 version.
- **`+excuses` added no signal** (28.6% vs ~25% base rate) across 49 flagged horses.

### Strategy Adjustments
- [ ] **Fix the MC class-normalisation defect.** Confirmed across R3, R4, R9, R10 — the composite rating correlates with official HKJC rating at r = +0.124 to +0.713 depending on class mix, and class drop-downs are systematically buried. This is the single highest-value fix available.
- [ ] **Stop overriding the banker on market coverage alone.** 0/2 this meeting. Require a model-side reason (a tie, as in R9) rather than a market-side one (R6).
- [ ] **Re-examine the `-injury30d` penalty against rule 9.** R2's winner was a 2.3 favourite penalised out of the pool on a vet clearance. Proposal: cap the penalty at −1/−2 when market odds ≤ 6.
- [ ] **Reconsider the "cover the whole pool = PASS" rule for small fields.** R3's realised dividend was 6× my modelled fair value. Where the second-string favourite is short (place ≤1.5), its failure inflates dividends far above the takeout loss.
- [ ] **Prefer the cheaper ticket when both cover the same top combinations.** R8: $60 vs $150 for the same hit.
- [ ] **Drop or re-weight `+excuses`** — no measurable edge over 49 observations.
- [ ] **Fix `fetch-odds.ts` wrap-around** which fabricated a phantom R11 duplicating R1's field.
- [ ] **Fix SCMP place-odds parsing** — corrupt in R6–R10 (place column duplicating win, or exceeding it).

### 11e. Session Verdict

**Strategy A won by $30, entirely on stake sizing — not on selection.** The two strategies chose the same banker in all ten races and hit the same two, so the meeting produced zero information about whether SCMP adjustments and race classification add value. A's edge came from Mode A's tighter 5-horse pool being $40 cheaper on R8, the one race that paid.

The session's real finding is upstream of the A/B question: **the banker held in only 3 of 9 live races (33.3%)**, and both discretionary banker overrides failed. Meanwhile the pre-race reports correctly identified the model's class-drop defect and named the exact horses it was burying — **R4 #1 AMAZING DUCK (2nd), R10 #1 PUBLIC ATTENTION (2nd), R9 #5 BABY SAKURA (won)** — without ever acting on that diagnosis in ticket construction. Closing that gap between diagnosis and selection is worth more than any refinement of A versus B.
